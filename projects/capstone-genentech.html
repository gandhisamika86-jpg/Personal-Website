▪ Overview
Developing a force-dose assist device that reduces subcutaneous and ocular injection forces for high-concentration biologic drug formulations.
01
Section 1
Problem Statement

As increasingly concentrated biologic formulations for intravitreal injections become more prevalent, the solutions also become more viscous. Due to the viscosity, injection forces during an administered dosage using standard off-the-shelf syringes increase, resulting in increased injection difficulty. There is a need for a device that can reliably integrate with syringes to reduce injection forces and ensure optimal injection form.

Needle gauge
30–34 G
Injection speed
100 μL / 10 sec
Compatibility
1 mL BD syringe
Use case
Multiple-use device

DHF 4
Defining the Engineering Problem

Research and stakeholder feedback were translated into the following engineering needs.

Need Category Specific Need
Compatibility
  • Support an injection rate of 0.01 mL/min with a 32G needle
  • Work with off-the-shelf 1 mL syringes
Safety
  • Must not increase silicone oil particulates
  • Must be sterilizable
Cost
  • Must be affordable
  • Must be eligible for insurance reimbursement
Performance
  • Must reduce injection force
  • Must be usable one-handedly and prioritize patient comfort
Ease of Use
  • Must not make the procedure more difficult
  • Must improve syringe and needle stability

DHF 5
Concept Generation & Down-Selection

We considered multiple concepts and evaluated them against the project needs using a Pugh matrix. The selected concept was a Scott Russell linkage.

Physics

The linkage converts the applied input into horizontal output force.

F₂ₓ = cot(θ)F₁ · Fₓ = F₂ₓ − fμ

As the linkage angle decreases, mechanical advantage increases. In the proof of concept, effective output exceeded the applied force at approximately 37.85°.

Pros
  • Provides mechanical advantage to support injection
  • Inexpensive to manufacture
Limitation

Provides more assistance near the end of the injection than at the beginning.

DHF 6
Solution Statement & Subsystems

The selected concept was divided into eight functional components.

ComponentFunction
Commercial SyringeProvides the standard 1 mL Luer-lock syringe and 32G needle interface.
Fixed Frame MountAnchors the linkage and stabilizes the syringe and needle.
Driving Lever ArmReceives the clinician’s one-handed thumb input.
Scott Russell Coupler LinkConverts the applied input into axial plunger-driving force.
Output SliderContacts and advances the syringe plunger.
Linear Guide RailConstrains the slider to one axis while maintaining alignment and limiting travel.
Sterilizable HousingEncloses the mechanism and supports validated sterilization.
Tapered NeedleGuides medication flow into the standard 32G lumen.
