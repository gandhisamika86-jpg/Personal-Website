01
Overview

Quality-System Integration for a Class III PMA Device

J&J acquired a company that manufactured and sold a Class III premarket approval (PMA) implant. My responsibility was to integrate the acquired company’s quality system into J&J’s quality system. Because the implant is a PMA device, new and revised risk documentation is submitted to the FDA. More specifically, I was responsible for overhauling the implant’s Application FMEA (AFMEA), Design FMEA (DFMEA), and Process FMEA (PFMEA).

02
FMEA Contents

Contents of an FMEA

Each FMEA documents the failure scenario, evaluates its risk, and identifies the controls used before and after launch.

Failure Mode Cause Effects Severity Rate of Occurrence Detection Rate Pre-Launch Risk-Mitigation Strategy Post-Launch Risk-Mitigation Strategy
03
My Role

My Role

To identify credible failure modes and effective risk controls, I observed surgical procedures, spoke with surgeons, reviewed manufacturing procedures and CAD drawings, ran mock surgical workflows, and analyzed post-market complaints. I also worked cross-functionally with packaging and labeling teams to understand how failures could occur and how they could be prevented or further mitigated.

I calculated occurrence ratings using defects per million opportunities (DPMO) and learned how the DPMO methodology differs when evaluating a reusable implant versus a single-use device.

Complaint Reporting

Overview of Complaint Reporting

Each month, complaints involving a range of medical products were analyzed and engineering recommendations were presented to leadership. During my internship, I evaluated and presented approximately seven field failures, translating complaint evidence into recommendations for the appropriate quality and engineering response.

This work strengthened my understanding of the differences among a nonconformance, nonconformance action, corrective and preventive action (CAPA), field recall, escalation, and other quality-system pathways.

Complaint Reporting Engineering Recommendations Leadership Presentations Nonconformance Nonconformance Action CAPA Field Recall Escalation
02
Complaint Investigation

Overview of Complaint Investigation

Complaints indicated that a tissue sealer was producing inadequate tissue seals. One potential cause was insufficient jaw force, so I investigated whether the trigger mechanism generated adequate force by attaching force sensors to the jaws and testing the device on porcine tissue.

The study included 30 devices, five operators, and two trials per device, producing 300 test observations. I compared jaw-force measurements with the lower and upper specification limits using Cp/Cpk analysis in Minitab. Both capability results were greater than 1.67, eliminating the trigger mechanism as the root cause.
